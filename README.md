<h3 align="center">Arbiter</h3>

<p align="center">Explainable deck decisions for Yu-Gi-Oh! and Pokémon. Build or paste a list, then read a score that says why it landed there: opening-hand odds, role counts, gaps, and specific swaps.</p>

<p align="center">A constraint solver can retune counts without inventing a new archetype. Two lists from the same game can be compared on consistency, interaction, and setup. Arbiter does not play the cards out, and it does not estimate a match win rate.</p>

<p align="center"><sub>FastAPI, Next.js, and Python. An unofficial fan tool. Not affiliated with Konami or The Pokémon Company.</sub></p>

<h3 align="center">DuoBrowser</h3>

<p align="center">A multi-screen web browser for the iPhone Duo, written in SwiftUI and WebKit. It keeps three independent sessions — cover, inner left, and inner right — with badges for which screen a page belongs to, a live map of the layout, and bookmarks that can restore every panel at once.</p>

<p align="center">An ephemeral mode clears cookies, storage, and history when the app leaves the foreground.</p>

<p align="center"><sub>iOS 17 and later.</sub></p>

<h3 align="center">Tokyo, 1.00 sq mi</h3>

<p align="center">One square mile of downtown Tokyo, centered on Shimbashi, as an interactive 3D model in a single HTML file.</p>

<p align="center">Building footprints, streets, parks, and the elevated expressway come from OpenStreetMap: about 4,400 buildings, not a procedural stand-in. Orbit the district, walk it at street level, or switch to a map, then click a building for its name and height.</p>

<h3 align="center">Tetris Retro</h3>

<p align="center">Tetris in one HTML file. Film grain, scanlines, procedural chiptune, and neon CRT blocks. It uses a 7-bag randomizer, hold, a ghost piece, and stores a best score in the browser. Keyboard on a desktop, swipe on a phone.</p>
